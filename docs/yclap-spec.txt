ADMU Problem Tree

Problem: Increasing climate stress on Ateneo’s extensive urban forest and its ability to remain resilient (Climate Stress on Ateneo’s Urban Forest)
Roots: 
	•	Increasing Infrastructure: Campus buildings, roads, and other structures can reduce available soil and growing space for trees.
	•	Policy: Climate resilience and native biodiversity as Ateneo’s platforms sometimes cannot compete with other priorities in long-term campus landscaping and development (i.e., prioritizing a car park over protecting the landscape).
	•	Awareness and Knowledge: Limited understanding of how tree diversity and native species can strengthen ecosystem resilience. Limited awareness of how increasing heat, drought, and extreme weather can stress urban forests.

Consequences:
	•	Comfortability: Less comfortable outdoor spaces for students and staff → higher stress, weaker communities, less productivity.
	•	Decreased Biodiversity: Native trees struggle to regenerate in areas dominated by Mahogany. Loss of habitat for birds, insects, and other wildlife. Reduced ecosystem resilience to heat, drought, and extreme weather.
	•	Socioeconomic Loss: Increased costs for tree maintenance and restoration. Fewer resources for climate-resilient landscaping and water-management technologies.
	•	Environmental Stress: With roughly ⅔ of the campus being green, Ateneo contributes significantly to urban green spaces in Manila. Reducing this further can worsen other climate risks and biodiversity loss in the country.


Solution:
	•	Improving student awareness of how significant green spaces are on campus.
	•	Strengthened involvement of student or environmental sectors in policy/decision-making
	•	Greater need to integrate climate resilience into campus development and landscaping policies.

	•	To develop a Native Tree Climate Resilience Plan by Q4 of AY 2026-2027 identifying priority planting areas, suitable native species, potential suppliers, planting and maintenance guidelines, invasive species management, and monitoring mechanisms.
	•	To establish a native tree monitoring system by Q4 of AY 2026-2027 to track the campus native tree population, new plantings, tree survival, and changes in species diversity.

Influences
	•	Climate stress: Increasing heat and changing rainfall affect Ateneo’s campus native trees.
	•	Campus development: Construction and landscaping affect tree cover and native species.
	•	Existing initiatives: AIS (Ateneo Institute of Sustainability) , researchers, and student groups, and campus offices already work on biodiversity, but coordination can be strengthened.
	•	Student population: Ateneo’s large student population offers strong potential for citizen science, although sustained participation depends on awareness and interest.
	•	Campus management: CFMO (Central Facilities Management Office) decisions influence tree planting, maintenance, removal, landscaping, and access to green spaces.
	•	Biodiversity data: Existing maps, inventories, and research can support the platform but require consolidation, validation, and regular updating.
	•	Digital engagement: The growing use of websites, mobile devices, and interactive platforms makes biodiversity information more accessible and can encourage wider student participation
	•	Environmental awareness: Growing concern about climate change and biodiversity can increase support for campus conservation efforts.

Story

https://www.scribd.com/document/983418313/Exer-3-Group1-SFI-107
Esmena, et al. (2025). “Assessment of urban green spaces per capita in a megacity of the Philippines: implications for sustainable cities and urban health management: A Journal Critique (Olfato-Parojinog et al., 2024)”. 

Human activities in NCR cause a severe historic deficit of urban green spaces along NCR, accounting for a 40% overall drop (Esmena et al., 2025). This resulted in further extreme heat hazards for people and disaster vulnerability. 

While most people have knowledge of the benefits of having access to green spaces, they’re not empowered to maintain or act upon them. With the proposal of a user-driven website, the The Ateneo community, including students, faculty, and staff, can learn to value the importance of biodiversity and engage them in monitoring the species.


Risks
	•	If game incentives rely solely on intrinsic environmental awareness, user turnout will remain minimal outside of already environmentally inclined student groups (such as environmental science majors and climate advocates). Without broad-based incentives and frequent promotional campaigns, this may lead to a quick drop in active users after launch.
	•	The group may face financial constraints in maintaining and improving the website as they expand the map.
	•	Because the scope of this project is within Ateneo campus, given that there is a limited initial set of featured trees, players may quickly explore all designated routes, scan every available species, and complete the main content in a short timeframe. Once players collect all available badges and exhaust the campus map, gameplay will stall, leading to high post-launch churn and loss of sustained user interest.
	•	Dead spots in cellular signals or unstable Wi-Fi in some of the forested/green areas within the campus may disrupt the students’ exploration and experience with the website.
	•	Users may experience technical difficulties since the website relies on location-based triggers and image scanning, leading to confusion due to possible inaccurate location tracking, slow loading, or failed scans.
	•	User-submitted data may contain inaccurate species identifications or observations, which could affect the reliability of the biodiversity database.
	•	Access restrictions to certain green spaces may limit the areas that can be mapped and included in the website’s exploration features.
	•	The website may become difficult to sustain after the project ends if there is no designated Ateneo office or organization responsible for its maintenance and updates.
