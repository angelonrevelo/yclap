What is for

Naming is the lever.

Climate stress on Ateneo’s urban forest has three roots: infrastructure taking growing space, policy in which biodiversity loses to other priorities, and the one students can actually act on — awareness.

A community that cannot name its forest is not a constituency for that forest when a car park is proposed over it.

Vision

An Ateneo where every student can name what they walk under, and where the campus forest has a constituency that shows up for it.

Mission
  • Make noticing a habit, not a poster — by making the walk itself the activity.
  • Produce what the campus inventory does not have: a species, a count and a coordinate, contributed by the people who walk past the tree.
  • Keep every number honest — rarity comes from real observation data, and where a figure is ours, the app says so.

How it plays

Walk to it. Photograph it. Keep it.

  • Finds are placed, not conjured. Every phone on campus in the same half-hour sees the same world.
  • Rarity is data. How rare a species is comes from its real iNaturalist observation count on this campus. Seen once reads “Once on campus”.
  • You must be there. A find within 40 metres opens the camera. You cannot log one from across campus.
  • Nothing ranks anyone. Thirteen badges, all earned by doing the thing the app is for. No currency, nothing purchasable, no leaderboard.
What a walk produces

Every logged find carries a species, a count, a coordinate, an accuracy and a time — exportable as CSV and GeoJSON. That pair, a count and a location, is exactly what the existing campus inventory does not have.

Photos and notes never leave the phone.
